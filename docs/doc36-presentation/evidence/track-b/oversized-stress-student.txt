          Two Pots, One Difference
          Two pots are set up to be compared. They hold identical soil, they are watered by the same
          amount, and they stand in the same room at the same temperature. The only difference be-
          tween them is where each pot sits.




                              window                                          dark cupboard
          The two seedlings are treated the same in every way except where they stand.

          A plant that adds height and unfolds new leaves is doing something that costs energy. That
          gives this lesson its question:

          Where does the energy a plant uses to grow actually come from?

          What you already know
                Plants are living things that need light and water to grow.
                Every living thing needs energy to stay alive.

          Two possible answers
          Where the growing energy comes from is exactly what people disagree about.

          Where the Growing Energy Comes From

            Key idea
            The energy a plant grows on is captured from light, not taken in from the soil.
          The green parts of a plant — mostly its leaves — absorb light energy. The leaf uses that en-
          ergy to make the plant's own food: a sugar built from water drawn up out of the soil and
          carbon dioxide taken in from the air.

            In one line
                                                                  light energy
                       water        +   carbon dioxide (CO2)          →          sugar (the plant's food)

          The energy stored inside that sugar is what the plant spends on building new leaves, stem
          and roots.

          Follow the energy, step by step
                Sunlight reaches the leaf.
                The leaf's green parts capture that light energy.
                The captured energy is used to build sugar from water and carbon dioxide. The energy
                is now stored inside food the plant has made for itself.
                The plant breaks some of that sugar down again and uses the released energy to build
                new leaves, stem and roots. That is growth.

            Notice two things
            The energy enters the plant through the leaf, never through the soil. The only things arriving from the
            soil are water and dissolved minerals. Leaf area can be measured in m2.


          So what is the soil for?
          Soil does a real job. It holds the plant firmly in place, and it holds water and dissolved min-
          erals, which the roots take in and which then travel up to the leaves. Minerals are raw mate-
          rials. They are not food, and they carry no usable energy.

           WHAT THE PLANT TAKES IN WHERE FROM                        IS IT ENERGY?

           Water                           Soil, through the roots No. A raw material for making sugar.
           Dissolved minerals              Soil, through the roots No. Raw materials for building the plant's body.
           Light                           Captured by the leaves Yes. It ends up stored in sugar.

          So a plant standing in rich soil with plenty of water, but kept in the dark, cannot make its
          own food and cannot grow, however well watered it is.


          Two Beliefs That Fail the Evidence

            Common belief 1
            The belief      “The soil feeds the plant. A plant kept in the dark will keep growing as long as it is
                            watered.”
            The evidence            Both pots held the same soil and got the same water. If the soil were supplying food
                                    and energy, the cupboard plant should have grown just as well. It stopped growing
                                    and turned pale.
            So                      Soil supplies only water and dissolved minerals. The plant's food is made in its own
                                    leaves, using light energy.
            Why did the cupboard plant keep going for a while? It was living on food it had already made and stored.
            Once that store ran down, it stopped growing and lost its green colour.


            Common belief 2
            The belief      “Light only keeps a plant warm or makes it look healthy. The energy for growth comes
                            from the water and the minerals.”
            The evidence            Both pots stood in the same room at the same temperature. The cupboard was not
                                    cold, so the cupboard plant lacked no warmth. It still did not grow.
            So                      Light is not merely warmth. The leaves capture light energy and use it to make the
                                    plant's own food, and growth runs on the energy stored in that food.
