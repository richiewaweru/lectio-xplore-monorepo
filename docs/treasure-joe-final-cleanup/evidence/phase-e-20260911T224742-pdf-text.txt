A life cycle is the series of stages an organism passes through as it grows and de-
velops. For a monarch butterfly, we can see this by following what happens from
egg to adult. The cycle begins with an egg, then moves through the caterpillar and
chrysalis stages, and finally becomes an adult butterfly. TJ-E-MARKER-

Looking back at our monarch butterfly near the milkweed plant, we can see this pat-
tern in action. The adult butterfly lands on a leaf and lays a tiny egg. That egg holds
all the instructions needed for a new monarch to grow. The egg hatches into a cater-
pillar that spends its time eating milkweed leaves so it can grow bigger.
After the caterpillar grows enough, it forms a chrysalis around itself. Inside this pro-
tective case, the caterpillar's body changes into a completely different form. When
the change is complete, an adult butterfly comes out. This adult can fly to find food
and a mate.
The monarch's life cycle repeats when an adult butterfly produces young. When that
adult butterfly lays eggs of its own, each egg starts the series of stages all over
again. Each new generation passes through the same stages: egg, caterpillar,
chrysalis, and butterfly.

Think about a path that has four steps: egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, butterfly. If the
path simply stops after the butterfly, we are looking at a line that comes to an end.
The butterfly is the last step, and then there is nothing new.
A life cycle is different because it does not stop at the butterfly. It still has the same
four stages: egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, and butterfly. But after the butterfly, the path
continues when the butterfly produces a new egg.
So, the line that stops goes egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, butterfly, then nothing. The life
cycle goes egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, butterfly, and then back to a new egg.
